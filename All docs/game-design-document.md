# Game Design Document (GDD)
## FPS Storytelling Game

**Project Type:** First-Person Shooter (FPS)  
**Genre:** Action | Tactical Shooter | Story-Driven  
**Perspective:** First Person  
**Primary Experience:** Single-Player Narrative Campaign  
**Development Engine:** Unreal Engine  
**Project Stage:** Concept Development & Pre-production  
**Document Status:** Initial Draft – Version 1.0

---

# 1. Game Vision

## 1.1 Core Vision

The objective is to develop an immersive, cinematic first-person shooter that combines engaging combat, strategic decision-making, realistic environments, and a compelling narrative.

The game will focus on delivering a memorable single-player experience in which the player is not simply completing missions but actively experiencing a larger conflict through the eyes of the protagonist.

The gameplay should balance intense action sequences with exploration, character interactions, tactical encounters, and story-driven moments.

## 1.2 Design Philosophy

The game will be developed around five core principles:

1. **Immersion:** Create believable environments, sound design, animations, and interactions that make the player feel present in the game world.

2. **Storytelling:** Every major mission, character, and location should contribute to the larger narrative.

3. **Meaningful Gameplay:** Combat encounters and objectives should encourage players to think, adapt, and make tactical decisions.

4. **Player Engagement:** Maintain a balance between action, exploration, suspense, and narrative progression.

5. **Quality Over Quantity:** Prioritise polished gameplay systems and memorable missions rather than building a large amount of unfinished content.

---

# 2. Target Player Experience

The player should feel like an active participant in a larger conflict rather than an observer following a sequence of events.

The intended experience includes:

- Immersive first-person combat.
- High-intensity action sequences.
- Tactical and strategic encounters.
- Exploration of detailed environments.
- Character-driven storytelling.
- Suspenseful mission progression.
- Cinematic narrative moments.
- A sense of consequence and progression.

## 2.1 Gameplay Experience

The overall gameplay should alternate between different types of experiences:

- High-intensity combat encounters.
- Slower tactical sections.
- Exploration and environmental discovery.
- Storytelling and character interactions.
- Stealth-oriented situations.
- Large-scale cinematic sequences.

The pacing should prevent the game from feeling repetitive.

---

# 3. Core Gameplay Loop

The core gameplay loop defines the activities the player repeatedly performs.

### Primary Gameplay Loop

1. Receive a mission briefing.
2. Prepare equipment and understand objectives.
3. Enter the mission environment.
4. Explore the surroundings and identify threats.
5. Engage enemies or complete objectives.
6. Adapt to changing mission conditions.
7. Complete the primary objective.
8. Experience the mission's narrative outcome.
9. Unlock the next mission or progression element.

Each mission should introduce new situations, challenges, or narrative developments.

# 4. Player Mechanics

## 4.1 Movement System

The movement system should feel responsive, controlled, and immersive.

Planned movement mechanics:

- Walking.
- Running and sprinting.
- Crouching.
- Prone movement (subject to design requirements).
- Jumping.
- Vaulting over obstacles.
- Climbing designated surfaces.
- Leaning around corners.
- Smooth transitions between movement states.

Movement speed, acceleration, and animations should be balanced to support both tactical and fast-paced gameplay.

## 4.2 Camera System

The game will use a first-person camera.

Planned camera features:

- First-person character perspective.
- Smooth camera movement.
- Weapon visibility and positioning.
- Camera recoil during firing.
- Environmental camera effects.
- Context-sensitive animations.
- Controlled camera transitions during cinematic sequences.

Camera effects should enhance immersion without making gameplay uncomfortable.

## 4.3 Interaction System

Players should be able to interact with relevant objects and elements within the environment.

Planned interactions:

- Opening and closing doors.
- Collecting equipment and supplies.
- Activating switches and controls.
- Interacting with mission-related objects.
- Accessing documents and information.
- Using environmental objects.
- Interacting with characters and mission elements.

An interaction prompt should appear when an object can be used.

# 5. Combat System

Combat is one of the central gameplay pillars.

The combat system should combine responsive shooting mechanics, tactical positioning, enemy behaviour, and weapon variety.

## 5.1 Shooting Mechanics

Planned shooting features:

- Hip-fire shooting.
- Aim-down-sights (ADS).
- Weapon recoil.
- Reloading.
- Ammunition management.
- Weapon switching.
- Fire-mode selection where appropriate.
- Bullet impact effects.
- Hit reactions.
- Weapon-specific firing behaviour.

Each weapon should have its own handling characteristics.

## 5.2 Damage System

The damage system should provide clear and consistent feedback.

Potential features:

- Player health.
- Enemy health.
- Damage calculation.
- Hit detection.
- Damage feedback.
- Death and respawn logic where appropriate.
- Health recovery mechanics.

The system should be designed around fair and understandable combat.

## 5.3 Combat Feedback

Combat should communicate important information through:

- Audio feedback.
- Visual hit indicators.
- Weapon animations.
- Impact effects.
- Enemy reactions.
- Environmental destruction where supported.

# 6. Weapon Design

Weapons should serve different gameplay purposes rather than functioning as interchangeable equipment.

## 6.1 Planned Weapon Categories

- Assault Rifles.
- Submachine Guns.
- Sniper Rifles.
- Shotguns.
- Light Machine Guns.
- Pistols.
- Special-purpose equipment.

These are proposed categories, not a confirmed final weapon list.

## 6.2 Weapon Attributes

Each weapon may have the following attributes:

- Damage.
- Fire rate.
- Accuracy.
- Recoil.
- Effective range.
- Magazine capacity.
- Reload duration.
- Handling speed.
- Mobility impact.

Weapon balancing should ensure that every weapon category has a meaningful role.

## 6.3 Attachments and Customisation

Potential customisation options:

- Optics and sights.
- Suppressors.
- Grips.
- Extended magazines.
- Barrels.
- Laser attachments.

Customisation should influence gameplay without creating unnecessary complexity.

# 7. Enemy AI System

Enemy AI should create believable and challenging encounters.

## 7.1 Basic Enemy Behaviour

Planned AI behaviours:

- Patrol designated areas.
- Detect the player.
- Investigate suspicious activity.
- Take cover.
- Engage the player.
- Search for the player after losing visibility.
- Communicate with nearby enemies.
- React to environmental changes.

## 7.2 Enemy Awareness States

The AI may use different awareness states:

1. Unaware – Enemy has not detected the player.
2. Suspicious – Enemy notices unusual activity.
3. Alerted – Enemy suspects a threat.
4. Engaged – Enemy actively fights the player.
5. Searching – Enemy searches for a player who is no longer visible.

## 7.3 AI Difficulty

Difficulty should influence enemy behaviour, reaction time, accuracy, and tactical decision-making.

Higher difficulty should challenge the player's decisions rather than simply making enemies unfairly accurate or excessively durable.

# 8. Mission Design

Missions will form the main structure of the single-player campaign.

## 8.1 Mission Structure

A typical mission may contain:

- Mission briefing.
- Insertion or starting sequence.
- Initial exploration.
- Primary objective.
- Secondary objectives.
- Combat encounters.
- Narrative developments.
- Extraction or mission conclusion.

Not every mission needs to follow the same structure.

## 8.2 Mission Objectives

Potential objective types:

- Reach a designated location.
- Secure an area.
- Rescue a character.
- Retrieve important information.
- Protect an objective.
- Disable a system.
- Investigate a location.
- Survive an encounter.
- Escape from a hostile area.
